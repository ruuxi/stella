# Brand And Showcase Surfaces

Use this for Stella-created apps or pages where the visual experience is part
of the deliverable: landing pages, portfolios, product showcases, editorial
surfaces, venue pages, game openings, campaign-like pages, and visual-first
experiments.

## Standard

The design needs a point of view. A brand surface that feels like a template
has failed even if it is neat. Name the intended lane before building:
physical references, audience, mood, typography, imagery, and color strategy.

## Typography

Choose type from the subject, not from design reflex:

1. Write three concrete voice words, such as "warm, mechanical, precise" or
   "coastal, spare, tactile".
2. Name the fonts or type styles you would reach for by reflex.
3. Reject reflex picks unless they are already part of the user's identity.
4. Pick type that feels like a physical artifact from the world of the brief:
   a receipt, museum caption, field guide, diner menu, lab label, concert
   poster, fabric tag, game manual, or architectural plaque.

Large expressive type is allowed, but it must fit the subject. Editorial serif
plus tiny mono labels is only right for editorial-shaped work, not a universal
shortcut.

## Color

Brand surfaces can use committed, full-palette, or drenched color strategies.
Do not retreat to muted neutral safety unless restraint is the named voice.

Before choosing colors, name a real-world reference: a product material,
place, era, lighting condition, art direction cue, or cultural object. Palette
should come from that reference.

## Layout

- Avoid default centered-stack pages.
- Use asymmetric composition, strict visible grids, immersive scenes, or
  section-by-section art direction when the brief supports it.
- One dominant idea per fold is often stronger than several same-weight cards.
- Cards are allowed only when they are the right affordance, not filler.

## Imagery

If the brief implies a real object, place, person, product, food, fashion,
travel, architecture, photography, or venue, ship imagery. A colored rectangle
where a photo belongs is not restraint.

Valid imagery includes:

- Local project assets.
- Verified stock photos.
- Generated raster assets.
- Product screenshots.
- Custom canvas, WebGL, or SVG scenes when they reveal the subject.

One decisive image is better than several vague ones. Use useful alt text that
matches the voice of the page.

## Motion

Brand surfaces may use more ambitious entrance, scroll, and typographic motion
than product UI, but the motion must support the story. Avoid scattered
micro-interactions that feel unrelated to the page concept.

## Brand Bans

- Zero imagery on an image-led brief.
- Large rounded icons above every heading.
- Repeated tiny uppercase labels above every section.
- Timid palettes when the brief needs identity.
- Generic editorial magazine styling on non-editorial subjects.
- Monospace as lazy shorthand for "technical".
